Four ways to see motion

One fixed camera over a parking deck. Two vehicles cross the aisle; nothing else moves. Four motion-detection algorithms score every frame — two of them the same greyscale frame-difference, once on the CPU and once on the GPU; one the NVIDIA hardware Optical Flow Accelerator; one MOG2 background subtraction. Scrub the clip and watch where they agree, and where they don't.

How the scores are built

One decode, four readers

sample_cam6.mp4 ships with a broken three-frame cadence — every third frame is a byte-duplicate of the one before it. It was de-duplicated to 80 unique frames (from 120) before anything else ran. Every algorithm then reads the identical luma plane of the identical frames, so any difference between them is the algorithm, not the pipeline.

Normalisation — two stages

Each raw score is first made resolution-independent: absdiff and MOG2 are per-pixel means and fractions already; the optical-flow magnitude is divided by the frame diagonal, so the same real motion scores the same at any resolution. Then each series is scaled to [0,1] by its own 1st–99th percentile range. That second step is clip-relative: the shapes and the crossings are comparable across the four algorithms, but a 0.8 here is not an absolute motion level. CPU and GPU share one set of scaling constants so their traces stay pixel-identical.

Thresholds

One threshold per algorithm, placed by Otsu's method on that algorithm's normalised histogram — the standard way to split one distribution into two classes. Each lands with strong separation (η > 0.8 for all four). A frame is motion when its normalised score is at or above the line.

What the four are

The clip

A 360° fisheye over a parking deck — parked cars around the rim, a transit aisle through the middle. It is deliberately not de-warped: correcting the lens first would be a fairer physical measurement but a less fair comparison, since the algorithms would no longer see identical pixels. Motion magnitude therefore varies with position in the frame. Scores are computed only inside the illuminated disc; the dead corners are masked out.
