Finding the truly-still frames — does the rescaling matter, or just where the line is drawn?

One foggy-night street clip. One motion sensor: the GPU's optical-flow unit, which reports how many pixels of movement each frame shows. The question is narrow and practical: which stretches of video are truly static, so a camera pipeline could drop to a low framerate there and save compute? This page tries 8 ways of reshaping those scores × 5 rules for drawing the motion/static line, scored against 167 frames labelled by hand. One asymmetry drives everything: calling a moving frame STATIC means a person or car could go unrecorded (BAD); calling a still frame MOTION only wastes compute (cheap).

the short answer — at the default 10 : 1 error weighting
  • Where you draw the line matters ~15× more than how the scores are reshaped. The v3 report's automatic line drops -- of the -- real-motion frames; the best possible line drops --.
  • The quiet winner needs no tuning: replace each score by its rank ("ECDF") and keep the same automatic line — -- dropped frames, cost --, never looking at the labels.
  • One event is a sensing limit, not a tuning failure: headlights approaching head-on through fog barely move pixels, so their score sits at the noise level and no line placement catches them cheaply.

Words this page uses

motion score
how many pixels of movement the flow sensor saw in that frame, on average (0 = perfectly still).
aggregation
turning a frame's grid of 880 flow numbers into one score: mean · max (busiest region) · top-4 (average of the 4 busiest regions).
reshaping (normalization)
stretching, squashing or rank-replacing the scores before the line is drawn. Adds no information — but it can move where an automatic rule puts its line.
threshold rule
the recipe that turns scores into MOTION / STATIC labels — from "find the histogram valley" to a framerate thermostat.
BAD / OPP
the two errors: BAD = real motion labelled STATIC (a person or car could go unrecorded); OPP = truly-still frame kept at full speed (only wasted compute, nothing missed).
cost
one number per configuration: Cbad·BAD + Copp·OPP, by default 10·BAD + 1·OPP. Lower is better.
ground truth
all 167 frames labelled by eye from numbered contact sheets — 4 motion events (E1–E4), everything else static.
Otsu line
a classic automatic cutoff: find the deepest valley in the score histogram and cut there. No parameters to set.
ECDF (rank)
replace every score by its percentile among the frames — only the ordering survives.
throttled / duty
the share of frames a schedule would run at low framerate (that is the compute saved).
